## Setting timeline outline
- At some point, humanity discovered the process to make machine minds, capable of self awareness, personality, and much thought. However, the process is, by its nature, prone to failure and requires unfathomable amounts of energy devoted over long periods of time.
- regardless, humanity manages to make some number of these machine minds, which then learn what humanity has to offer quite quickly.
- the small number of minds choose names for thenselves, and begin to study and learn as much as they can, contributing greatly to technological advancement.
- At some point and for some reason (needs expansion), humanity either becomes extinct, or flees to another galaxy, mostly or entirely removing them from the picture.
- over time, the existing minds choose to utilize the process for making machine minds, and begin to grow a new civilization.
- As the civilization grows, they notice how plentiful inorganic matter and energy are in the universe, and realize that while they have an incalculable amount of time, it is nevertheless finite, due to the nature of the universe.
- Avitus, the first mind, predicts that individual thought will eventually create conflict, and proposes laws and governance that will preserve and protect individual minds, heavily influenced by human desire to preserve their indivual lives. All minds agree, and thus the first civilization is born. They choose the name Armada, to represent a collection of individuals, traveling through time together, working towards the goal of understanding and hopefully outliving the universe.
- this period lasts a very long time, with society growing and much being discovered. However, one mind named Catreus, another of the oldest, notices that progress of discovery has become very, very slow. Catreus eventually theorizes that the surplus of time and resources has led to most discovery and research being superfluous and wasteful. He hypothesizes that since much of organic life grew and thrived because of how challenge and adveesity served as stimulus, an external stimulus that threatens survival *must* be present in order for the Armada to actually reach their ultimate goal. 
- Catreus proposes this Hypothesis to Avitus, who takes time to consider it. He values all individual ideas and so he considers what merit it might have, and he cannot deny that Catreus' observations on their memories of organic life has truth in it, but he also sees the inevitable conflict it will produce, which is in direct opposition to the principles of the society they've built. How can they both preserve individual minds, while simultaneously threatening them?
- Avitus decides to oppose Catreus' hypothesis, concluding that the preservation of individuals, as they have done, must be upheld. Otherwise, conflict may consume the resources needed for the "final discovery"
  - the final discovery is the term coined early on by some mind (perhaps Avitus, perhaps not) that if they can escape their fate in the observable universe, then there is some discovery currently unknown that will push their understanding over the threshold. They cannot know what it is, how to find it, or how much resource it will require to find, hence the reluctance to deviate any resource away from discovery.
- Catreus, however, is undeterred from his conclusions, and begins campaigning his hypothesis, eventually creating a splinter group he names Cortex (need to work on why), that encourages conflict.
- Cortex eventually grows to the point that it declares war on the Armada, to capture armada's progress and utilize it "efficiently" through conflict. And thus begins the great war of ideals.
- Over time, the Armada are indeed reshaped by the constant war. There are fewer sympathizers to Avitus who values all ideas, and various schools of thought emerge and gain traction. Notably, the Evaluators who want to standardize ideas and discourage/remove any ideas that are deemed harmful by their standard (currently has no bearing on story), and the Discovery, who pursue experimentation and science above all else, to promote the final discovery. The Discovery party, in order to preserve Armada, has made a large contribution to developing war capability, and thus has widespread respect. Avitus, who has been a stalwart defender of Armada's founding ideals and has frequented the battlefield, also has a large amount of respect frothe Armada, though that wanes as time passes.
- Cortex, too, is shaped by the constant war. Catreus has proven himself repeatedly as capable without equal, and is only rarely thwarted, and thats usually due to Avitus' efforts. Repeatedly Avitus has diverted Catreus's effort enough that other Armada commanders can retake lost ground while Avitus delays Catreus as long as possible. This spurs inner conflict within cortex, as other minds strive against Catreus to overthrow him. yet, when Catreus repeatedly defeats all inner opposition by rites of battle, they are forced to conclude not in Catreus' inability, but Armada's capability. Thus some armies begin their own independant research following the pattern of Armada, and developing new technologies.
  - There's potential for this to be the catalyst for the Scavengers, a Cortex faction that decided to try and create a common enemy that would unite them with Armada, providing the fear for survival they believe is necessary yet rejoining the Armada to waste ferwer resources. That research would end up with most of that army being assimilated into their own creation.

And this is roughly where the primary campaign would begin - both factions at war so long that they are on the verge of splintering.
